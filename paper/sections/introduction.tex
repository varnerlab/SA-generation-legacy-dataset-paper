\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% Motivation: small clinical datasets, maternal mortality, coagulation in pregnancy
Pregnancy induces profound changes in the coagulation and fibrinolytic systems,
shifting the hemostatic balance toward a hypercoagulable state \citep{Grouzi2022}.
Pregnant and postpartum women face a 4--5 fold increased risk of venous
thromboembolism compared with non-pregnant women, and between 2017 and 2019,
thrombotic embolisms caused 8.7\% of pregnancy-related deaths in the United States,
while hemorrhage caused 13.7\% \citep{Trost2022,Harris2023}. Understanding
patient-specific coagulation dynamics during pregnancy is therefore critical for
risk stratification and clinical decision-making.

However, longitudinal clinical datasets capturing the full coagulation, fibrinolysis,
thrombin generation, and viscoelastic profiles across pregnancy are rare and expensive
to collect. Typical cohort sizes are on the order of tens of patients, far too small
for training machine learning models or performing robust statistical analyses.
Synthetic data generation offers a path forward: by augmenting small real datasets
with statistically faithful synthetic records, we can enable downstream computational
analyses that would otherwise be infeasible.

% Previous approach and its limitations
In prior work, we generated synthetic patient populations using multivariate normal
distributions fitted independently to each clinical visit \citep{Varner2023JuliaCon}.
While this approach captured first- and second-order statistics, it assumed Gaussian
marginals and linear correlations, and it generated each visit independently rather
than as part of a coherent longitudinal trajectory.

% SA and its connection to Hopfield networks
Stochastic Attention (SA) provides an alternative framework rooted in modern Hopfield
network theory \citep{Ramsauer2021}. Modern Hopfield networks define an energy
landscape over continuous state vectors:
\begin{equation}
    E(\vxi) = \frac{1}{2}\norm{\vxi}^2
    - \frac{1}{\beta}\log\sum_{k=1}^{K}\exp\left(\beta\,\mathbf{m}_k^\top\vxi\right)
    \label{eq:hopfield_energy}
\end{equation}
where $\{\mathbf{m}_k\}_{k=1}^K$ are stored memory patterns and $\beta$ is an inverse
temperature parameter controlling the sharpness of retrieval. By running Langevin
dynamics on this energy landscape, SA generates novel samples that interpolate between
stored patterns in a manner governed by the attention mechanism.

% What we do in this paper
In this work, we adapt SA for continuous clinical data and longitudinal patient
trajectories. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We formulate a pipeline for applying SA to continuous multivariate clinical
    measurements, including a direction-magnitude decomposition that preserves
    anisotropic variance structure lost by unit-norm projection.
    \item We introduce concatenated-visit patterns that treat each patient's full
    longitudinal profile as an atomic unit, enabling SA to generate coherent
    multi-visit trajectories.
    \item We demonstrate that SA-generated synthetic patients faithfully reproduce
    summary statistics, correlation structure, and biologically meaningful pregnancy
    trajectories from a 72-feature, 3-visit coagulation dataset.
    \item We compare SA against multivariate normal generation, showing improved
    preservation of non-Gaussian features and nonlinear dependencies.
\end{enumerate}
