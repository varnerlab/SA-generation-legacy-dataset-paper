Small longitudinal clinical datasets pose a fundamental challenge for computational modeling
and machine learning in biomedical research. We present a method for generating synthetic
patient data using Stochastic Attention (SA), a sampling framework based on modern Hopfield
networks. We apply SA to a longitudinal dataset of coagulation, fibrinolysis, thrombin
generation, and viscoelastic measurements collected from pregnant patients across three
clinical visits (pre-pregnancy baseline, first trimester, and third trimester). Our approach
concatenates multi-visit patient profiles into unified patterns, applies PCA for dimensionality
reduction, and uses Langevin dynamics on the Hopfield energy landscape to generate novel
synthetic trajectories. A direction-magnitude decomposition preserves the natural variance
structure of continuous clinical measurements. We demonstrate that SA-generated synthetic
patients faithfully reproduce per-feature summary statistics (mean relative error $<3\%$),
cross-feature correlation structure, and the characteristic pregnancy-driven shift in the
Factor~X versus Maximum Clot Firmness (MCF) plane. Compared with multivariate normal
sampling, SA better preserves non-Gaussian marginal distributions and nonlinear
inter-feature dependencies while generating clinically plausible novel patient trajectories.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} synthetic data generation, stochastic attention, modern Hopfield networks,
coagulation, pregnancy, longitudinal data
